# Design note

**Pipeline:** fixed-camera video → SH17 YOLOv8s at 1280 → ByteTrack → native-head-crop MobileNetV3-small → readable/violating/compliant observations → persistence and episode deduplication → JSONL, snapshots and evidence clips. This Markdown page is the submission design note.

**Measured tradeoffs.** On the Intel Core Ultra 7 256V (8 cores/8 threads), CPU only, the detector is OpenVINO INT8 at 736×1280 on all 8 threads: 8.0 processed FPS over six SteelBench clips (detector 114ms), with the same events as PyTorch CPU (2.95 FPS). INT8 calibration uses 15 cameras outside the evaluation set. Smaller input and the nano detector lose helmets and vests, so neither is used. Frame skipping: with nobody seen for 1s only 2 FPS is processed (83% of a person-free low-light clip skipped); 8 FPS sampling keeps up with a live camera (1.14× real time) with no lost event; 5 FPS loses one. Historical M2 CoreML results are hardware-specific. At 3.2 samples/s a worker moving 1.4m/s moves 0.44m/sample; narrow zones remain risky.

**Evidence and identity.** ByteTrack preserves low-score recovery; dev replay selected confidence fusion off and a 5s lost buffer. Additional-camera identity changes fell 7→0; one-ID people rose 3→6 of 7. Coverage remains 56.9%, so detection misses dominate. Four of 19 labelled workers are never tracked. Approximate hand-box diagnostics do not establish MOT benchmark accuracy. Hidden predictions never supply PPE evidence. Long gaps reset pending violation clocks; new alerts require current readable violation evidence. PPE uses a 2s/70% vote, 1.5s persistence, 0.6s maximum evidence gap, 3s episode expiry and 60s cooldown.

**Failures and behaviour.** Workshop: seated workers merge behind machinery; train on partial people rather than stretching geometry matches. Distant/hazy ladle workers are barely detected, causing missed entries. Night/IR: heads under 18px abstain; IR remains untested. Head wraps/hoods and white helmets need in-domain crops; the classifier helps but cannot certify the site's false-alert budget. Reflective coveralls still produce false vest alerts. Hidden feet use decaying remembered height and remain uncertain. Camera movement pauses zones against a saved reference; reflections/posters remain unvalidated. Development event recall is 11/14; additional cameras 6/10 with one false alert and no duplicates in the subsequent full-inference assessment. Do not equate fewer alerts with better quality.

**Scaling and setup.** At the measured 8.0 processed FPS, this CPU sustains one camera at 5–8 sampled FPS; 100×5 / 8.0 ≈63 such CPUs before overhead and before idle skipping, not a deployment sizing recommendation (the same laptop's iGPU: 22 FPS). Use camera substreams, an accelerator and measured batched multi-camera capacity first. Decode, inference, tracking and evidence encoding stay local; only events and selected media go upstream (roughly 50MB/day at 30 alerts using earlier ~1.5MB evidence bundles). Offline uplink buffering/retry is planned, not built; idempotent event delivery needs durable IDs and a disk queue. New cameras need only reviewed polygons, a saved reference and a per-camera configuration, then a silent validation shift. No automatic safety-policy inference.

**Operator trust.** Collect confirmed/dismissed alerts and audit non-alerted tracks. Use reviewed mistakes as hard negatives, calibrate per camera and lighting condition, monitor abstention/dismissal drift, and revalidate after camera movement. Do not auto-adjust thresholds from dismissals alone: missed events also matter. CMOT is skipped by owner instruction. The README/audit list reproducible commands, incomplete live-system work and remaining submission fields.
